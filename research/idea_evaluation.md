# Candidate evaluation

Source: `Sentiment Analysis Research Directions.pdf`, 28 pages, read in full by text extraction. This is an idea document, not an independently verified literature review. Its numerical hypotheses are proposals, not evidence. Some cited titles/authors do not match the linked primary sources (for example, the linked UncertainNLP paper is by Aneesh Durai). Its claims of first applications are not adopted.

Available resources: AMD Ryzen 5 5500U, 12 logical CPUs, approximately 14 GiB memory; no NVIDIA GPU detected. All choices below are specific to this environment.

Additional ideas in the document (multi-causal attenuation, noise memorization, ordinal distance, negation scope, domain-shift coverage) are mechanisms underlying these ten candidates, rather than distinct experimental proposals.

## Selection

Select candidate 10, narrowed to **ordinal error versus probability calibration in CPU-trained, TF-IDF sentiment classifiers**. Use one official dataset at two label budgets, three seeds, an existing ordinal log-loss baseline, a cumulative-threshold baseline, label smoothing, and temperature scaling. The contribution is a reproducible controlled evaluation, not a new loss or a claim about transformer fine-tuning. Prior literature already covers ordinal log-loss on SST-5 (Castagnos et al., COLING 2022), so the PDF's broad novelty claim is rejected. A symmetric distance penalty is used because no defensible asymmetric misclassification cost is supplied.

Why this over candidates 3 and 8: it directly tests both prediction severity and probability quality without expensive pretrained trajectories or unverifiable coverage guarantees. Accessible official sentence labels and small sparse models permit complete controls and saved predictions. A negative result is interpretable. Single-corpus and representation limitations will be explicit.
